Thin-slice CT provides ﬁner anatomical depiction, but in some clinical settings, only thick-slice CT
images are available, resulting in reduced visible anatomical detail. Simply resampling to a smaller
through-plane spacing cannot alleviate partial-volume-related image degradation, motivating deep
learning-based CT slice synthesis (CSS) as an auxiliary strategy. Here, we release Multi-ratio Slice
Synthesis CT (MSS-CT), a multi-ratio real-paired dataset comprising thick-slice and 1 mm CT
reconstructed from identical raw acquisitions, covering 3 mm-to-1 mm (500 pairs) and 5 mm-to-1 mm
(1500 pairs) synthesis. Using MSS-CT, we developed BasicCSS as a reference model, evaluated it
against representative CSS methods in a multicohort setting, and compared real-paired with pseudopaired training. BasicCSS achieved the highest performance in internal and external testing, whereas
pseudo-paired training led to marked declines across all evaluated methods, consistent with
mismatch between simulated thick-slice inputs and clinically reconstructed thick-slice CT.
Downstream consistency analyses showed that BasicCSS-synthesized 1 mm CT had higher
consistency with real 1 mm CT-derived segmentation and radiomic outputs than conventional
interpolation. In a two-radiologist reader study, synthesized 1 mm CT reviewed alongside original
5 mm CT as an auxiliary view received high usefulness and conﬁdence-support scores. These results
suggest that AI-generated thin-slice CT may serve as an auxiliary image when real thin-slice CT is
unavailable, and that MSS-CT and BasicCSS enable reproducible benchmarking for future CSS
development and evaluation under clinically reconstruction conditions.
Computed tomography (CT) is a core diagnostic imaging modality, and its
clinical and quantitative value depends in part on how volumetric information is reconstructed and represented1,2. During CT reconstruction, raw
projection data are reconstructed into a series of two-dimensional slices
according to predeﬁned parameters, such as slice thickness, slice interval,
and reconstruction kernel, yielding a three-dimensional volumetric representation. Among these parameters, slice thickness is a key determinant of
spatial resolution and image ﬁdelity. Thin-slice CT provides denser axial

sampling, improving through-plane resolution and anatomical
delineation3–5. In routine practice, however, the availability and use of thinslice CT may be limited by image-noise and dose trade-offs, storage burden,
and health-system speciﬁc cost considerations6–8. Thick-slice CT therefore
remains widely used, despite reduced through-plane resolution and blurring
from partial-volume effects, in which mixed tissue components within a
voxel reduce image contrast and structural sharpness9. These slicethickness-related effects can inﬂuence visual image interpretation and

Conventional interpolation is commonly used to resample thick-slice
CT to a thinner slice spacing, but it standardizes image sampling without
alleviating partial-volume-related image degradation caused by thick-slice
reconstruction. To address this limitation, deep learning-based CT slice
synthesis (CSS) has been developed to generate thin-slice CT from thickslice inputs when thin-slice reconstruction is desired but unavailable12–21.
Existing CSS methods can be broadly grouped into reformatted-view superresolution12,15,21, volume super-resolution13,14, and slice-wise interpolation
paradigms16–20, which differ in how they model through-plane information.
Although recent CSS methods have shown promise, their development or
evaluation has often relied on thick-slice inputs simulated by artiﬁcially
degrading thin-slice CT19–21. Such pseudo-paired data provide a practical
surrogate when real-paired reconstructions are unavailable, but they only
approximate the reconstruction differences observed between real thickslice and thin-slice CT series. It therefore remains uncertain whether CSS
models trained on pseudo-paired data can preserve information needed for
image interpretation and quantitative analysis when applied to real thickslice inputs.
This uncertainty reﬂects a central data limitation in current CSS
research, as large-scale real-paired datasets with thick-slice and thin-slice CT
series reconstructed from identical raw acquisitions remain limited. Without such data, evaluation may be shaped by simpliﬁed degradation
assumptions rather than by scanner-speciﬁc reconstruction characteristics,
slice-thickness-related blurring, and other differences observed in clinical
CT reconstruction. A publicly available real-paired dataset has provided an
important step toward realistic CSS evaluation16, but its limited scale and
single synthesis ratio restrict systematic assessment across different synthesis difﬁculties. A larger multi-ratio real-paired dataset is therefore needed as
a common data foundation for developing CSS methods and evaluating
their performance under realistic reconstruction conditions.
With such a data foundation, CSS evaluation can extend beyond visual
performance to whether synthesized thin-slice CT supports downstream
quantitative image analysis and clinical image review. For quantitative
image analysis, the value of synthetic thin-slice CT depends on whether it
can produce outputs that are more consistent with those derived from real
thin-slice CT22–24. In anatomy-based quantitative assessment, ready-to-use
segmentation tools increasingly provide anatomical measurements and
downstream quantitative outputs from CT images25–27. Variations in slice
thickness, however, can affect segmentation performance and reduce the
consistency of segmentation-derived measurements28–30. In feature-based
quantitative assessment, radiomics provides another common pathway for
extracting CT information as quantitative features describing intensity and
texture patterns31. Radiomics pipelines commonly use interpolation-based
resampling to standardize voxel spacing32, but this step does not resolve the
information loss caused by thick-slice reconstruction33–35. Alongside these
quantitative endpoints, synthesized images should also be evaluated for
whether they provide information that supports human interpretation
rather than visual realism alone36. For synthesized thin-slice CT, its practical
value may depend on whether it can support CT image interpretation when
only thick-slice CT is available.
To address this data gap and enable CSS evaluation beyond image
similarity alone, we release Multi-ratio Slice Synthesis CT (MSS-CT), a
large-scale, multi-ratio, real-paired chest CT dataset. MSS-CT includes 2000
subjects and supports 3 mm to 1 mm and 5 mm to 1 mm synthesis using
thick-slice and thin-slice series reconstructed from identical raw acquisitions (Fig. 1a). We further developed BasicCSS as a reference model and
benchmarked it against representative CSS methods and conventional
interpolation across internal and external real-paired datasets (Fig. 1b). The
evaluation assessed synthesis ﬁdelity, the effect of real-paired versus pseudopaired training, downstream structural and radiomic consistency, and the
perceived value of synthesized 1 mm CT as an auxiliary view (Fig. 1c). By
making MSS-CT and BasicCSS publicly available, this study further

Results
Study cohorts
MSS-CT was constructed as a real-paired chest CT dataset for CT slice
synthesis, with paired thick-slice and 1 mm CT series reconstructed from
identical raw acquisitions. As shown in Table 1, MSS-CT included 2000
subjects across two internal subsets. MSS-CT 3 mm supported the 3 mm to
1 mm synthesis task, and MSS-CT 5 mm supported the 5 mm to 1 mm
synthesis task (Fig. 1a). MSS-CT was used for model development and
internal evaluation, whereas two independent real-paired datasets, RPLHRCT16 and EC-CT, were used for external testing of the 5 mm to 1 mm task
(Fig. 1b). Across all paired datasets, the thicker-slice CT series was used as
input, and the matched real 1 mm CT series was used as the reference.
Internal CT slice synthesis performance on MSS-CT
We compared BasicCSS with 16 representative methods across the three
main CSS paradigms under identical real-paired training and testing settings. These comparators included reformatted-view super-resolution12,15,21,
volume super-resolution13,14, slice-wise interpolation16–20, and representative
natural image super-resolution and video frame interpolation methods
adapted to CSS37–42. In the main text, ACVTT21, ResVox13, and CTHNet18
are reported as representative comparators for the three paradigms, with
complete quantitative results for all 16 methods provided in Supplementary
Tables S1 and S2. Synthesis performance was evaluated against the matched
real 1 mm CT reference using PSNR and SSIM measured in the axial,
coronal, and sagittal planes.
BasicCSS achieved the highest PSNR and SSIM in all three planes for
the 3 mm to 1 mm synthesis task (Fig. 2a). Trilinear interpolation showed
lower performance across PSNR and all three SSIM metrics, with a PSNR of
37.97 ± 0.93 dB and axial, coronal, and sagittal SSIM values of 0.967 ± 0.005,
0.964 ± 0.006, and 0.963 ± 0.006, respectively. All comparisons between
BasicCSS and the evaluated methods were statistically signiﬁcant for each
metric, with all p < 0.001 (Supplementary Table S1).
For the 5 mm to 1 mm synthesis task, BasicCSS again achieved the
highest PSNR and three-plane SSIM (Fig. 2b). BasicCSS reached a PSNR
of 44.09 ± 1.43 dB, compared with 42.11 to 43.48 dB for the other CSS
methods. The performance gap between methods was larger in the
higher-ratio 5 mm to 1 mm task, particularly relative to trilinear interpolation. All comparisons between BasicCSS and the evaluated methods
were statistically signiﬁcant for each metric, with all p < 0.001 (Supplementary Table S2).
Qualitative comparisons supported the quantitative ﬁndings. In
selected examples, CSS methods reduced residual blurring relative to trilinear interpolation, although remaining ﬁne-structure loss was more evident in the 5 mm to 1 mm task than in the 3 mm to 1 mm task. Highcontrast structures such as bones were preserved by most CSS methods.
Among the compared methods, BasicCSS more consistently preserved ﬁne
airway walls and small vessels and showed less residual blurring, particularly
in the 5 mm to 1 mm task (Fig. 2c, d).
Ablation experiments supported the contributions of the block-wise
decoder and cross-view reﬁnement module (Supplementary Table S3 and
Supplementary Fig. S1). Each component improved synthesis performance
and convergence, and the full BasicCSS model outperformed an enlarged
block-wise decoder variant without cross-view reﬁnement, suggesting that
the gain was not explained by increased model capacity alone.

External testing for the 5 mm to 1 mm synthesis task was performed on two
independent real-paired datasets, RPLHR-CT and EC-CT, to assess generalization beyond the internal MSS-CT data. The main text reports the
same representative comparators as in the internal evaluation, and complete
quantitative results for all 16 methods are provided in Supplementary
Tables S4 and S5.

Across all four datasets, CSS-CT showed higher radiomic feature
consistency with real 1 mm CT-derived features than thick-slice CT and
Tri-CT, as reﬂected by CCC distributions and reproducible feature proportions. Tri-CT showed a different pattern, with lower overall mean CCC
than thick-slice CT but a higher proportion of reproducible features. This
apparent discrepancy may reﬂect the feature-dependent and nonuniform
effect of trilinear interpolation. Compared with thick-slice CT, Tri-CT
tended to show a wider CCC range, suggesting that interpolation changed
radiomic features in inconsistent directions and to different extents. Consequently, some features exceeded the reproducibility threshold of 0.85,
whereas reduced concordance in other features lowered the overall
mean CCC.
Radiomic feature consistency differed across feature groups. The
advantage of CSS-CT over thick-slice CT and Tri-CT was most evident
for LoG features, whereas wavelet features had lower CCC values and
fewer reproducible features across datasets, suggesting greater sensitivity
to dataset and reconstruction differences. Radiomic consistency also
varied by synthesis ratio and dataset. MSS-CT 3 mm showed higher
consistency than MSS-CT 5 mm, consistent with the lower synthesis
difﬁculty of the 3 mm to 1 mm task. Among the 5 mm to 1 mm datasets,
EC-CT showed a proﬁle closer to MSS-CT 5 mm, whereas RPLHR-CT
had lower absolute radiomic consistency, particularly for wavelet features. These ﬁndings suggest that radiomic consistency after CSS

characteristics.

Reader study
The reader study assessed whether BasicCSS-synthesized 1 mm CT could
provide added value when reviewed with original 5 mm CT as an auxiliary
view. Sixty EC-CT cases, including 30 healthy cases and 30 pneumonia
cases, were independently reviewed by two radiologists. For each case,
original 5 mm CT and synthesized 1 mm CT were displayed side by side
without access to real 1 mm CT. Readers were blinded to health status.
Guided by CT image-quality criteria that emphasize diagnostic information
and visible anatomical detail43, the readers assessed added information,
usefulness, and conﬁdence support. Added information was rated as yes or
no, and usefulness and conﬁdence support were scored on 5-point scales.
Usefulness and conﬁdence-support ratings were high and similar between
healthy and pneumonia cases (Fig. 7 and Table 2). Across 120 reader-case
ratings, the mean usefulness score was 4.83, and the mean conﬁdencesupport score was 4.69. No reader assigned scores of 1 to 3 for either item.

A central motivation for MSS-CT was to bring CSS development and
evaluation closer to the input conditions expected in clinical use. Pseudopaired training remains attractive because it can convert existing thin-slice
CT into surrogate paired data, but its validity depends on how closely the
simulated thick-slice input approximates clinically reconstructed thick-slice
CT. Real thick-slice CT reﬂects through-plane signal aggregation, partialvolume blurring, and scanner-speciﬁc reconstruction characteristics, which
are only partly captured by slice sampling or interpolation-based degradation. In our experiments, pseudo-paired training produced lower performance than real-paired training, with a larger decrease in the sampled
pseudo-paired setting than in the degraded pseudo-paired setting. These
results suggest that real-paired data remain important for verifying synthesis
performance and method improvements. The ﬂexibility of pseudo-paired
training also allows studies to use different source datasets and pseudo-pair
generation strategies, which may complicate method comparison and limit
consensus on relative CSS performance. MSS-CT was therefore released as a

The MSS-CT benchmark showed lower performance across methods
in the 5 mm to 1 mm task than in the 3 mm to 1 mm task, consistent with
the greater through-plane spacing and stronger partial-volume averaging in
5 mm inputs. Conventional interpolation performed worse because
resampling can standardize slice spacing but cannot recover information
lost during thick-slice reconstruction. Across both synthesis settings, slicewise interpolation methods generally achieved higher synthesis performance than reformatted-view super-resolution and volume superresolution methods. This pattern is consistent with the CSS task, in which
missing intermediate slices are mainly inferred from the local relationship
between adjacent thick-slice inputs. These observations suggest that explicit
modeling of axial relationships remains important for CSS under realpaired reconstruction conditions.
BasicCSS achieved consistently higher synthesis performance than the
comparator methods on the MSS-CT internal test sets. Relative to the
strongest comparator, CTHNet, BasicCSS improved PSNR by 0.29 dB in the
3 mm to 1 mm task and by 0.61 dB in the 5 mm to 1 mm task. These

improvements were larger than the corresponding PSNR gaps between
CTHNet and the next-ranked methods. Visual comparisons showed less
residual blurring and more stable preservation of ﬁne airway walls and small
vessels, particularly in the more challenging 5 mm to 1 mm task. Ablation
results supported the contributions of the block-wise decoder and crossview reﬁnement module. These ﬁndings are consistent with the design
rationale of BasicCSS. In thick-slice CT, each reconstructed slice reﬂects
through-plane signal aggregation and partial-volume blurring, and the
missing intermediate 1 mm slices are mainly constrained by the local
anatomy between adjacent thick-slice positions. The block-wise decoder
was designed to reﬁne these local axial regions, making the synthesis task
more localized and tractable. The cross-view reﬁnement module was
applied after axial feature expansion and block-wise decoding, when the
feature representation is closer to the target thin-slice space than to the
original thick-slice input. This allows sagittal and coronal continuity to be
reﬁned using reconstruction-ready axial features, which is particularly
relevant for structures affected by through-plane blurring, such as small
vessels and airway walls.

independent real-paired conditions, as it maintained the highest PSNR and
three-plane SSIM on both RPLHR-CT and EC-CT. Absolute performance
differed between the two external datasets, with lower image-similarity
metrics on RPLHR-CT than on EC-CT. This difference should be interpreted in the context of dataset-speciﬁc image acquisition, reconstruction

Prior studies have shown that slice thickness can affect segmentationderived measurements28–30 and radiomic features33–35. CSS has also been
explored as a way to improve radiomic feature reproducibility33 and support
automated CT image analysis18,22,23. Our study extends this evidence by
evaluating whether synthetic thin-slice CT generated from real thick-slice
inputs can produce structural and radiomic outputs that are more consistent
with those derived from real 1 mm CT. Across multicohort datasets,
BasicCSS-generated 1 mm CT showed higher consistency with real 1 mm
CT-derived structural and radiomic outputs than conventional interpolation. The structural analysis suggested greater beneﬁt for structures affected
by partial-volume-related degradation, although small tubular structures
such as lung vessels remained difﬁcult to recover completely. In the radiomic
analysis, BasicCSS improved feature consistency, with stronger preservation
of LoG features than wavelet features. This pattern may indicate that CSS
may better restore local density transitions and edge-related structures than
multiscale texture patterns, which remain more sensitive to reconstruction
differences. These ﬁndings indicate that downstream quantitative consistency is an informative endpoint for CSS evaluation, while the remaining

gaps in small structures and reconstruction-sensitive features identify targets for future method improvement.
Previous CSS and virtual thin-slice CT studies have used reader-based
assessments to evaluate image quality, anatomical visibility, or diagnostic
performance14,18,44. Our reader study instead focused on an auxiliary-view
setting, assessing whether synthesized 1 mm CT could provide useful
additional information when reviewed alongside the original 5 mm CT.
This setting reﬂects a practical scenario in which real thin-slice CT is unavailable, but an additional synthesized thin-slice view may help readers
review image information affected by partial-volume averaging. BasicCSSgenerated 1 mm CT was perceived as useful and conﬁdence-supporting by
both radiologists, including in cases with visible lung abnormalities. Because
BasicCSS operates on reconstructed CT images, the synthesized 1 mm series
can be generated after routine CT reconstruction and reviewed with the
original 5 mm CT as an auxiliary image. In an inference-time test, BasicCSS
completed synthesis within 1 min per CT volume on a single NVIDIA
Quadro RTX 4000 GPU with 8 GB VRAM, suggesting that its computational requirements are feasible for potential clinical use.

Overall model architecture and training
BasicCSS was designed as a reference model for real-paired CT slice
synthesis. It uses a thick-slice CT volume as input and generates a synthesized 1 mm CT volume. The same architecture was used for the 3 mm to
1 mm and 5 mm to 1 mm synthesis tasks, with ratio-speciﬁc settings for
different axial expansion factors. As shown in Fig. 8a, BasicCSS consists of a
volumetric encoder, a slice expansion module, a block-wise decoder, a crossview reﬁnement module, and a reconstruction tail. The slice expansion
module expands the feature volume along the axial direction to the target
1 mm resolution. The block-wise decoder reﬁnes local axial feature blocks
anchored by adjacent thick-slice positions. The cross-view reﬁnement
module further reﬁnes sagittal and coronal continuity before ﬁnal reconstruction, as illustrated in Fig. 8b. The 2D and 3D Swin-Conv blocks used in
the encoder and decoder are shown in Fig. 8c.
As part of preprocessing, CT intensities in all input volumes were
clipped to [−1024, 2048] HU. The intensity-clipped volumes were then
linearly scaled to [0, 1]. The model was optimized using L1 reconstruction
loss between the synthesized 1 mm CT volume and the matched real 1 mm
CT reference. Optimization used AdamW45,46 with a learning rate of 0.0001
and a batch size of 1. During training, input volumes of 8 × 128 × 128 voxels

Synthesis performance evaluation
Synthesis performance was evaluated for trilinear interpolation, BasicCSS,
and the comparator CSS methods on the MSS-CT 3 mm and MSS-CT
5 mm internal test sets. For the 5 mm to 1 mm synthesis task, external
evaluation was also performed on RPLHR-CT and EC-CT. Models trained
on MSS-CT 5 mm were directly tested on the two external datasets without
ﬁne-tuning. Each synthesized or resampled 1 mm CT series was compared
with the matched real 1 mm CT reference. Performance was assessed using
PSNR calculated over the whole CT volume and SSIM calculated separately
in the axial, coronal, and sagittal planes. Representative cases from all four
test datasets were selected for qualitative comparison. Detailed deﬁnitions
for PSNR and SSIM are provided in Supplementary Note 6.
Real-paired and pseudo-paired training comparison
To assess the effect of training-pair realism, real-paired training was compared with two pseudo-paired training strategies on the MSS-CT 3 mm and
MSS-CT 5 mm datasets. The comparison included BasicCSS and representative methods from the three main CSS paradigms, including ACVTT21

for reformatted-view super-resolution, ResVox13 for volume super-resolution, and CTHNet18 for slice-wise interpolation. Two recent pseudo-paired
methods, I3Net19 and SFCLI-Net20, were also included.
In the real-paired setting, the model input was the clinically reconstructed thick-slice CT series. In the sampled pseudo-paired setting, pseudo
thick-slice inputs were generated by sampling slices from real 1 mm CT at
ﬁxed axial intervals corresponding to the target synthesis ratio. In the
degraded pseudo-paired setting, pseudo thick-slice inputs were generated
from real 1 mm CT by axial degradation to simulate thick-slice inputs. Apart
from input construction, all settings used the same data splits, model
architecture, loss function, optimizer, training schedule, and data augmentation. After training, all models were evaluated under real-paired test
conditions using real thick-slice CT as input and matched real 1 mm CT as
the reference. Additional implementation details are provided in Supplementary Note 7.
Segmentation-derived structural consistency analysis
Segmentation-derived structural consistency was evaluated to assess whether synthesized 1 mm CT produced downstream anatomical segmentation
outputs consistent with those derived from real 1 mm CT. This analysis was
performed on the complete MSS-CT 3 mm internal test set, the complete
MSS-CT 5 mm internal test set, the predeﬁned 100-case RPLHR-CT test set,
and 100 randomly selected EC-CT cases. TotalSegmentator (v2.11)25 was
applied with default settings to matched real 1 mm CT, trilinear
interpolation-resampled 1 mm CT (Tri-CT), and BasicCSS-generated
1 mm CT (CSS-CT). Masks derived from real 1 mm CT were used as
reference segmentations, and Tri-CT- and CSS-CT-derived masks were
compared with the reference masks using the Dice similarity coefﬁcient.
Additional details on the evaluated structures and Dice calculation are
provided in Supplementary Note 8.
Radiomic feature consistency analysis
Radiomic feature consistency was evaluated to assess whether synthesized
1 mm CT generated quantitative image features consistent with those
extracted from real 1 mm CT. This analysis was performed on the same four
datasets used for the segmentation-derived structural consistency analysis.
For each case, a lung ROI deﬁned on real 1 mm CT was used for feature
extraction from real 1 mm CT, thick-slice CT, trilinear interpolationresampled 1 mm CT (Tri-CT), and BasicCSS-generated 1 mm CT (CSSCT). Radiomic features extracted from real 1 mm CT were used as the
reference, and the concordance correlation coefﬁcient (CCC) was calculated
for each feature across cases. A feature was considered reproducible if its
CCC was at least 0.85. Additional details on ROI sampling, feature extraction, and CCC calculation are provided in Supplementary Note 8.
Reader study
A reader study was performed to assess whether BasicCSS-generated
1 mm CT could serve as an auxiliary view when only original 5 mm CT
was available. The assessment focused on diagnostic information,
visible anatomical detail, usefulness for image review, and support for
interpretation conﬁdence rather than visual appearance alone, in line
with established CT image-quality criteria43. Sixty EC-CT cases were
randomly selected, including 30 healthy cases and 30 patients with
pneumonia. Two radiologists with 10 and 3 years of experience independently reviewed original 5 mm CT and BasicCSS-generated 1 mm
CT side by side. The real 1 mm CT was not shown because the study was
designed to evaluate the synthesized image as an auxiliary view rather
than as a replacement for real thin-slice CT. Readers were aware of
which series was original and which was synthesized, but were blinded
to health status and quantitative evaluation results. Readers evaluated
added information, usefulness, and conﬁdence support. Added information was rated as yes or no, and usefulness and conﬁdence support
were scored on 5-point scales. Additional details on the reading procedure, rating criteria, and deﬁnition of added information are provided
in Supplementary Note 9.

Statistical analysis
Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation unless
otherwise speciﬁed. For box plots, the center line indicates the median, and
the box indicates the interquartile range. For quantitative bar plots, bars
show mean values and error bars indicate standard deviation. For stacked
rating bars in the reader study, bars show score distributions. Paired comparisons were performed using two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests because the compared methods, training settings, or image types were
evaluated on the same cases or the same radiomic features. When multiple
pairwise comparisons were performed, p values were adjusted using Bonferroni correction within the corresponding comparison family.
For synthesis performance evaluation, BasicCSS was compared with
each comparator method using case-level PSNR and three-plane SSIM. For
the real-paired and pseudo-paired training comparison, case-level PSNR
and three-plane SSIM were compared between training-pair settings within
each synthesis task, method, and metric. In this analysis, real-paired training
was compared separately with each pseudo-paired training strategy.
For segmentation-derived structural consistency analysis, Dice values
were calculated for each case and anatomical structure. CSS-CT and Tri-CT
were compared for each anatomical structure within each dataset, using the
same real 1 mm CT-derived reference masks. Dice values are reported as
percentages in the ﬁgures and tables.
For radiomic feature consistency analysis, the concordance correlation
coefﬁcient (CCC) was calculated for each radiomic feature across cases,
comparing thick-slice CT, Tri-CT, or CSS-CT with the matched real 1 mm
CT reference. CCC distributions were compared at the feature level, with
pairing deﬁned by the same radiomic feature across image types. The
proportion of reproducible features, deﬁned as features with CCC ≥ 0.85,
was summarized descriptively.
Reader study results were summarized descriptively. Added information was reported as n (%). Usefulness and conﬁdence-support scores were
summarized by score distribution and mean score, using all ratings from the
two readers. Subgroup summaries were reported for healthy and pneumonia cases.
Statistical signiﬁcance was indicated as * for adjusted p < 0.05, ** for
adjusted p < 0.01, and *** for adjusted p < 0.001. All statistical analyses were
performed in Python 3.9.
